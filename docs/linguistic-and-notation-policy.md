# Kheper linguistic and notation policy

**Status:** Approved by maintainer self-review as lead Egyptologist
**Backlog ticket:** `KHEPER-010`  
**Policy version:** 0.1.0  
**Prepared:** 25 August 2026

## Purpose and authority

This policy fixes the linguistic and notation boundaries used by Kheper's MVP
content, authoring tools, validation, and learner interface. It keeps the
language being taught, the writing system, scholarly transliteration,
pedagogical pronunciation, visual presentation, and recommended construction
sequence separate.

This document is an implementation draft. It does not approve any individual
translation, transliteration, sign value, historical statement, monumental or
Hieratic form, pronunciation, or construction sequence. Those claims require
item-level sources and the review gates defined by the product charter.

## MVP language and script scope

- The initial linguistic target is Middle Egyptian. Content from another
  language stage may appear only as explicitly labelled context and cannot be
  assessed as though it were Middle Egyptian.
- The MVP teaches monumental hieroglyphic and reviewed Hieratic forms. It does
  not teach Demotic, Coptic, Ptolemaic specialist writing, or arbitrary
  inscription translation.
- A Hieratic form is related to a hieroglyphic sign through an explicit authored
  relation. It is not generated by applying a font or a generic transformation
  to a monumental form.
- Every authentic example records its language stage, script, period or date
  range, source, provenance, confidence, and review state. Unknown values are
  labelled unknown; they are not inferred for publication.

## Required separation of representations

Content and UI must store and label these as distinct values:

| Representation | Meaning | Must not be used as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Language stage | The linguistic variety represented by the content | A visual script or font |
| Script | Monumental hieroglyphic or Hieratic presentation | The language itself |
| Transliteration | A modern scholarly notation of Egyptian writing | A pronunciation or translation |
| Pedagogical pronunciation | A disclosed classroom convention, where supplied | A certain reconstruction of historical speech |
| Visual form | A reviewed monumental or Hieratic rendering | Sole evidence for sign identity or linguistic value |
| Recommended construction sequence | A reviewed teaching aid for reproducing a form | A universal historically correct ancient stroke order |
| Literal gloss | A close mapping that exposes the source structure | A fluent translation |
| Natural translation | Readable English rendering | A word-for-word gloss or transliteration |

Learner-facing screens must name the representation when two or more could be
confused. Changing UI direction must not change inscription reading direction,
and changing inscription direction must not change the stored sign identity.

## Transliteration convention

Kheper uses the Gardiner 1957-style Egyptological convention represented by
Unicode characters for its canonical transliteration fields. For the MVP
uniliteral inventory, the canonical consonant symbols are:

`ꜣ ꞽ y ꜥ w b p f m n r h ḥ ḫ ẖ s š ḳ k g t ṯ d ḏ`

The lead Egyptologist must approve each authored value and any use beyond this
inventory. Canonical values are stored as Unicode NFC. Rendering must use a
bundled font or another tested local asset with the required transliteration
coverage; missing glyphs must not silently replace canonical symbols.

Transliteration is displayed independently from a sign image and from any
pedagogical pronunciation. Capitalization, punctuation, brackets, damage or
restoration notation, morphological separators, and alternative readings are
not normalized silently. Any such notation requires a documented authored
meaning and specialist review before learner-facing use.

### Plain-keyboard input

The learner may enter this reversible ASCII fallback when the specialist
symbols are unavailable:

| Canonical | ASCII input | Canonical | ASCII input |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ꜣ` | `A` | `ꞽ` | `i` |
| `ꜥ` | `a` | `ḥ` | `H` |
| `ḫ` | `x` | `ẖ` | `X` |
| `š` | `S` | `ḳ` | `q` |
| `ṯ` | `T` | `ḏ` | `D` |

All other MVP consonants use their canonical ASCII letter. Matching converts
the fallback to canonical symbols before assessment, preserves the learner's
original input for local attempt evidence, and never treats uppercase and
lowercase as generally interchangeable outside this table. The input help must
be available before the first assessed use and remain accessible without a
network connection.

This fallback is an input method, not an alternative scholarly display scheme.
Learner feedback shows the canonical transliteration alongside the entered
fallback when a distinction matters.

## Pronunciation

- Audio or pronounceable spellings are optional pedagogical conventions.
- Before first use, learner-facing text states that Egyptian writing generally
  omits vowels and that the supplied pronunciation is a modern teaching aid,
  not a recording or certain reconstruction of ancient speech.
- Pronunciation data is stored separately from transliteration and cites the
  convention used. It cannot be derived implicitly by inserting vowels into a
  transliteration.
- Pronunciation quality cannot determine whether a transliteration, reading,
  or construction answer is linguistically correct in the MVP.

Required short disclosure:

> Pronunciation in Kheper is a modern learning convention; the exact ancient
> pronunciation is not known.

Any longer explanation or language-specific wording remains learner content
and requires linguistic review.

## Sign identity, Unicode, and catalogue codes

- Kheper's stable content ID is the durable internal reference. A Gardiner code,
  Unicode scalar, source-catalogue identifier, and displayed glyph are separate
  fields and are never substituted for the stable ID.
- A Gardiner code is shown as a modern reference label. It is not presented as
  an ancient name, pronunciation, meaning, or unique universal taxonomy.
- Unicode identifies encoded text elements and interchange values. Kheper does
  not rely on the operating system's hieroglyph font coverage for canonical
  learner presentation.
- Unicode/Unikemet catalogue properties have an explicit Unicode data version.
  Informative or provisional properties are labelled as such in provenance and
  cannot silently overwrite reviewed Kheper content.
- A sign without an applicable Unicode scalar or Gardiner code remains valid
  when it has a stable Kheper ID, reviewed identity, and bundled vector asset.
- Catalogue collisions, aliases, legacy characters, alternate sequences,
  mirroring, and rotation are resolved explicitly in authored metadata.

## Direction, orientation, and grouping

- Store UI direction, inscription layout direction, reading order, sign-facing
  direction, and glyph orientation independently.
- Determine and teach reading direction from reviewed inscription evidence. A
  general facing-sign cue may be taught, but it must not override an authored
  reading order for an exception or ambiguous example.
- Mirroring for a right-to-left layout is presentation behavior, not a change
  to the stable sign ID. Sign-specific no-mirror or rotation constraints must be
  authored and reviewed.
- Preserve quadrat/group structure as structured layout data. Do not encode a
  group as an unexplained string, flatten it into reading order, or treat its
  visual balance as decoration.
- Unicode Egyptian Hieroglyph Format Controls may represent interchange layout,
  but Kheper's deterministic bundled vectors remain authoritative for learner
  rendering and drawing evaluation.

## Variants and paired forms

- Teach one explicitly labelled canonical pedagogical form first. Record
  monumental variants, Hieratic forms, chronological forms, orientation
  variants, and graphical alternates as separate reviewed relationships.
- A visual variant does not automatically create a new linguistic value, and a
  shared linguistic value does not make two visual forms interchangeable for
  drawing assessment.
- Accepted construction alternatives are enumerated in the construction
  profile and described only as recommended construction sequences.
- Do not score a learner against an unreviewed variant. If a form is visible but
  not accepted for production, explain that distinction without calling it
  historically wrong.

## Glossing and translation layers

When applicable, a reading record keeps the following layers independently:

1. source sign/group representation and authored reading order;
2. canonical transliteration;
3. normalized Egyptian, if supplied;
4. literal gloss;
5. natural English translation;
6. notes on uncertainty, restoration, alternatives, and convention.

Omitted layers remain absent rather than being copied from another field. A
natural translation cannot serve as an answer key for transliteration, and a
literal gloss cannot be presented as idiomatic English. Alternatives carry
their own source, confidence, and review state.

## Confidence and publication rules

Use the authored confidence values `high`, `medium`, and `low`, each accompanied
by a short rationale and source reference. Confidence describes the reviewed
claim, not reviewer seniority. It does not replace a review decision.

- Draft and in-review claims may appear only in authoring or staging contexts.
- Rejected claims remain in changelog/audit evidence but cannot enter a
  production bundle.
- Production linguistic content requires an approved linguistic review record,
  attributable reviewer, review date, sources, version, and unchanged approved
  fields.
- A change to a linguistic field, its source, or its confidence returns the
  affected record to review. Later editorial-workflow tickets operationalize
  this rule; no tool may weaken it.

## Sources and version pinning

The policy relies on:

- The Unicode Standard, Version 16.0, Chapter 11.4, *Egyptian Hieroglyphs*, for
  encoded sign identity, directionality, mirroring, quadrats, and Egyptian
  Hieroglyph Format Controls:
  <https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode16.0.0/core-spec/chapter-11/>.
- Unicode Standard Annex #57, Version 17.0 Revision 5, *Unicode Egyptian
  Hieroglyph Database (Unikemet)*, for versioned catalogue properties,
  transliteration function-value notation, variants, mirroring, rotation, and
  property status: <https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr57/tr57-5.html>.

These sources define encoding and catalogue conventions; they do not by
themselves approve Kheper's linguistic interpretation of a sign or example.
Item-level content must follow the separate sourcing standard from KHEPER-013.

## Review checklist

The lead Egyptologist must record a decision for every item below:

- Middle Egyptian and MVP script boundary;
- canonical transliteration inventory and normalization;
- plain-keyboard mappings and assessment equivalence;
- pronunciation disclaimer and separation;
- Gardiner-code terminology and Unicode boundaries;
- direction, mirroring, orientation, and grouping wording;
- monumental/Hieratic relationship and variant rules;
- transliteration, normalization, gloss, and translation separation;
- confidence vocabulary and reopening triggers;
- source adequacy and any required corrections.
